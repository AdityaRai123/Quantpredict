The question

Predict one bit per day, and score it honestly

For every trading day t, the target is a single label:

Targett= 1 (UP) if Closet+1 > Closet0 (DOWN) otherwise

Daily direction is famously hard to predict. Prices react to news that is not in yesterday’s chart, and any pattern that worked reliably would be traded away. So the goal was never a magic model. It was a fair comparison: eight very different models and fifteen ways of combining them, all scored on the same unseen days, with every tuning choice made inside the training years.

The original project studied Reliance Industries in depth. This site runs the identical pipeline on all 49 usable NIFTY‑50 stocks, so each conclusion can be checked across the whole index instead of one company.

  1. RQ1Do six classical models, an LSTM and a Vision Transformer beat the always-guess-the-majority baseline?
  2. RQ2Does a Vision Transformer that reads 20-day feature matrices as images add value?
  3. RQ3Do soft, weighted, adaptive or stacked ensembles beat plain majority voting and the best single model?
  4. RQ4Does leakage-safe feature selection improve on the full 37-feature set?

Method

One pipeline, eighteen steps, no look-ahead

The same script, Quantpredict.py, ran unchanged for every stock. Step numbers below match the code.

  1. STEP 1–2aLoad & cleanParse NSE daily files, drop bad rows, and undo fake crashes from bonus issues and splits.
  2. STEP 3–437 featuresMACD, RSI, ADX, Bollinger, ATR, OBV, moving-average distances, volatility, lags. All stationary.
  3. STEP 5Split by timeFirst 80% of days train, last 20% test. Scaler fitted on training rows only.
  4. STEP 6Select featuresMutual information and tree importance pick a Top-20, judged by walk-forward F1.
  5. STEP 7–9Train 8 modelsSix classical classifiers, an LSTM on 20-day windows and a patch-based Vision Transformer.
  6. STEP 10BWalk-forwardFour expanding folds inside the training years give honest out-of-fold predictions.
  7. STEP 11–14VoteMajority, soft, F1-weighted, adaptive weighted voting and stacking, with weights from validation only.
  8. STEP 15–18Score & auditEvery row scored on the same test days, then 18 automated leakage checks.

The eight models

Each keeps its colour in every chart on this page.

    The voting rules

    Majority
    One vote per model; UP if more than half say UP.
    Soft
    Average the models’ P(UP); UP if the average is at least 0.50.
    Weighted
    Weights proportional to each model’s walk-forward validation F1, fixed for the whole test.
    Adaptive
    Weights recomputed every day from each model’s accuracy over the last W known days, sharpened by β. W and β are tuned on the training period.
    Stacking
    A logistic-regression meta-model learns how to combine out-of-fold probabilities.

    “7” ensembles use six classical models + LSTM. “8” adds the Vision Transformer and is marked experimental.

    How the timeline is split

    Walk-forward folds live entirely inside the training years. Voting weights, feature sets and adaptive settings are all chosen there. The test period is touched once, for scoring.

    Why prices had to be repaired first

    NSE files hold raw traded prices. A 1:1 bonus issue halves the price overnight, which looks like a −50% crash, creates a false DOWN label and breaks every indicator around it. The pipeline flags a day as a corporate action only when both the open and the close fall below 0.78× the previous close, snaps the gap to a clean ratio such as 1/2 or 1/5, and rescales all earlier rows. Real crashes, like March 2020, open near 0.90× and are left alone. Across the dataset it found … such events in … stocks.

      Limits

      Read the numbers with these in mind

      • One test period per stock. About 1,000 days is a small sample. Differences under roughly ±3 accuracy points are within the noise that chance alone produces.
      • “Best” is picked after the fact. The best single model and the best ensemble per stock are chosen by looking at test scores, so they flatter the method. Compare fixed rules, like majority or adaptive voting, for a fair read.
      • No trading costs. Accuracy here ignores brokerage, taxes, slippage and position sizing. A 52% hit rate is not a profitable strategy by itself.
      • Historical data only. The dataset stops on 30 April 2021. The next-day calls are what the models would have said then, not predictions for today.
      • Run details. This batch ran the deep models on the PyTorch backend with fixed seeds. Small differences from a TensorFlow run in Colab are expected.
      • Not financial advice. This is an academic study of model comparison, not a recommendation to buy or sell anything.
